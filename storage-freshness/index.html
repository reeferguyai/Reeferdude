Storage & Freshness: Keep It Right For Months

Flower is a perishable wearing a tough reputation. Stored badly it is harsh and weak in a month; stored right it improves for weeks and holds for a year. This guide covers the whole storage stack for flower, concentrates and edibles — and the myths that ruin more stash than any thief.

The four enemies

Light degrades THC, heat accelerates everything bad, air oxidises terpenes away, and excess moisture invites mold. The defense is boring and total: airtight glass, cool and dark, humidity held at 58–62% with a two-way pack, opened only when used.

Method by product

Flower: jars, 62%, dark cupboard, burp early jars. Concentrates: silicone or glass, cool, some swear by the fridge for rosin (sealed, and let it warm before opening or moisture condenses in). Edibles: follow food rules, label potency and date, freeze for the long term.

Myths that ruin stash

The freezer is not automatically good (trichomes go brittle and snap off with handling), plastic bags are not storage (static steals trichomes, air leaks), and the orange peel trick trades moisture control for mold risk. The humidity calculator and the freshness dates you write on the jar beat every folk remedy.
