Hemp Products Explained: What The Plant Gives

Hemp is one plant wearing many hats: fiber, food, oil, and cannabinoid extracts, all from Cannabis sativa bred and regulated for low THC. This page untangles the product families so you can tell what a label is actually promising — and what it legally cannot.

Fiber, seed and oil

Industrial hemp fiber becomes textiles, rope, paper and building material. Hemp seeds and hemp seed oil are foods — protein, omega fats, zero meaningful cannabinoids, no effect beyond nutrition. These products are legal almost everywhere and need no special shopping rules.

CBD and cannabinoid products

Extracts are where regulation lives. Hemp-derived CBD products are legal federally in the US under the 2018 Farm Bill framework, but rules differ by state and product type, and a major change is scheduled: from December 11, 2026, federal law caps total THC at 0.4mg per container for hemp products. The legal guide tracks this properly.

What hemp is not

Hemp is not marijuana — legally, hemp is cannabis at or below 0.3% delta-9 THC by dry weight (with the new per-container cap coming). Products that blur that line in their marketing are telling you something about their maker, and the fact-check page shows how to read them.
