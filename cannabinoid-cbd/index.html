CBD: Cannabidiol

Cannabidiol — CBD — is one of the four cannabinoid pages at the core of the learning library. Plain language, sourced claims, no shelf-copy promises.

What it is

CBD is the non-intoxicating cannabinoid that built the hemp industry: it does not produce a high, and it is researched for anxiety, pain, inflammation and epilepsy — with one FDA-approved drug (Epidiolex) for specific seizure disorders and a large body of preliminary evidence beyond it.

How it differs

Unlike THC, CBD barely binds CB1/CB2 directly; it works partly by slowing the breakdown of your own endocannabinoids. It also moderates THC — balanced and CBD-rich products feel steadier than pure-THC equivalents for many people, which is why ratios (1:1, 2:1) matter on labels.

The honest notes

CBD is a drug in the pharmacological sense: it is processed by liver enzymes (CYP450) shared with many prescriptions, including blood thinners. If you take medication, that conversation belongs with your clinician. Quality varies wildly in this market — lab results (COAs) are the difference between a product and a promise.
