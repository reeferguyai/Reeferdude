Germination Guide: Wake The Seed Up Right

Germination is the first skill of growing and the cheapest to learn: a viable seed, warmth, moisture and darkness are the whole recipe. This guide compares the three standard methods and names the handling errors that kill more seeds than bad genetics ever will.

The methods

Paper towel: seeds between damp (not wet) towels in a warm dark place, taproot in 24–72 hours, then into medium root-down. Water glass: 12–18 hours soak until they sink, then towel or medium. Direct sow: seed straight into its first pot, slowest feedback, zero transplant shock. All three work; towel gives the most visibility for beginners.

The killers

Drowning (soaking past a day), cold (under 65°F stalls everything), handling the taproot with fingers (oils and damage — use tweezers on the shell), and planting too deep — a quarter inch is plenty, the seed carries days of food, not weeks.

Legality and patience

Germination is cultivation in the eyes of most laws — check yours before a single seed gets wet. And give seeds their week: some old or stubborn genetics take 7–10 days. The grow guides by stage pick up from the moment the seedling stands up.
