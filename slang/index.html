Slang Dictionary: Talk The Talk

Every era names the plant anew, and the names are a history of their own: jazz-age reefer, beatnik grass, hippie weed, hip-hop loud, dispensary-era top-shelf. This dictionary defines the living slang and the museum pieces alike, with etymology graded by the fact-check standard — known, likely, or pure porch legend.

How entries work

Each entry gives the term, its meaning, its era and region where known, and a usage line. Slang moves fast and dates faster; entries get review dates, and the community submits additions through the submit page — new slang earns its place by being heard in the wild, not by being coined in a meeting.

Families of terms

The plant (herb, ganja, Mary Jane, chronic, loud), the session (toke, puff, session, cyph), the quality ladder (schwag, mids, top-shelf, zaza), the people (budtender, plug, heads). The glossary covers the technical vocabulary; this page covers the street's.

Respect the roots

Much of this vocabulary comes from Jamaican, African-American and Chicano culture — the dictionary credits origins where they are known, because slang without credit is just theft with better PR. The culture rules on the community rules page apply here in full.
