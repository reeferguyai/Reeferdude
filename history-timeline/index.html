Cannabis History: 10,000 Years In One Scroll

The plant is older than farming's written records and its legal status is younger than the airplane. This timeline walks the whole arc — ancient hemp industries, sacred and medical uses across cultures, the twentieth century's prohibition turn, and the modern legalisation era — each entry dated and sourced through the fact-check system.

The deep past

Hemp fiber appears in archaeological record over 10,000 years ago; cannabis medicines run through Chinese, Indian, Egyptian and Scythian practice for millennia. For most of human history the plant was infrastructure (rope, sail, paper) and medicine cabinet at once — the timeline restores that proportion against the modern fixation.

The prohibition century

1930s America: the Marihuana Tax Act (1937) strangles the industry in paperwork; 1970's Controlled Substances Act files cannabis as Schedule I — the category for substances deemed without medical use — where federal law has kept it since, even as states rebuilt legal markets beneath it. The timeline shows the votes, the dates and the money, sourced.

The modern turn

1996: California's Proposition 215 opens medical. 2012: Colorado and Washington vote adult-use. 2018: the Farm Bill splits hemp from marijuana in federal law. The 2020s: state markets mature while federal reform stalls and re-threads — including the hemp rules tightening in December 2026 tracked on the legal guide. History here is a live document; new entries land as they happen.
