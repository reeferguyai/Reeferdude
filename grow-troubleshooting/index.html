Grow Troubleshooting: Read The Plant, Fix The Problem

Plants cannot talk, but they are honest: nearly every grow problem writes itself on the leaves days before it becomes serious. This guide works symptom-first — what you see, what it usually means, what to check in what order — because panic-feeding a random nutrient is how small problems become big ones.

The check order

Before any diagnosis: pH, then environment (temperature, humidity, light distance), then watering pattern. These three cause the overwhelming majority of 'deficiencies' — a pH lockout starves a plant sitting in plenty of food. Fix the environment before touching the feed bottle.

Common sights

Lower leaves yellowing evenly in late veg: often nitrogen hunger or simple age. Burnt tips climbing the leaf: overfeeding. Spots with halos: calcium or a pathogen — the pest pages have the close-up comparisons. Drooping after watering that recovers by morning is thirst theater; drooping that stays is roots in trouble.

When to ask

Photograph the whole plant and the worst leaf, note your pH, feed and environment numbers, and bring it to the budtender chat or consultation. Diagnosis without numbers is astrology; with them, it is usually a five-minute fix.
