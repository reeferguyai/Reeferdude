Seed Buying Guide: Genetics Without The Guesswork

Seed listings are a dialect of their own: regular, feminized, autoflower, F1, landrace, 'fast version'. This guide translates every term, explains what actually drives price, and names the marketing tricks that separate new growers from their money.

The three families

Regular seeds grow male and female plants — you sex them yourself; cheapest per seed, the only route to breeding. Feminized seeds are bred to grow female almost always — the home grower's default. Autoflowers bloom on age instead of light schedule, fast and forgiving, smaller and less potent on average.

What you are paying for

Genetics work: stable lines cost years of selection, and that labor is the price. A famous name on unstable seed is worth less than an unglamorous stable line. Look for germination policies, harvest data and real lineage — the strain library teaches the reading of all three.

Scams and red flags

Seeds sold by the gram like flower, 'guaranteed' famous strains at flea-market prices, and any seller who cannot state lineage or test results. Also: know your local law before ordering anything — seeds are restricted in some places even as souvenirs.
